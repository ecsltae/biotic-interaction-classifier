## Q3(a). Pair-conditioned encoding in biomedical RE benchmarks and in PURE

### peng2019transfer
- Title: Transfer Learning in Biomedical Natural Language Processing: An Evaluation of BERT and ELMo on Ten Benchmarking Datasets
- Venue: Proceedings of the 18th BioNLP Workshop and Shared Task (ACL), 2019, pp. 58-65. Anthology W19-5006, DOI 10.18653/v1/W19-5006.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/W19-5006.bib and full PDF https://aclanthology.org/W19-5006.pdf
- WHAT IT SHOWS: BLUE benchmark (DDI, ChemProt, i2b2 2010 for RE). It injects the pair by entity blinding, one instance per pair: "We treated the relation extraction task as a sentence classification by replacing two named entity mentions of interest in the sentence with predefined tags (e.g., @GENE$, @DRUG$) (Lee et al., 2019). For example, we used "@CHEMICAL$ protected against the RTI-76-induced inhibition of @GENE$ binding." to replace the original sentence "Citalopram protected against the RTI-76-induced inhibition of SERT binding." in which "citalopram" and "SERT" has a chemical-gene relation." [full text, Sec. 4.1.2 "Fine-tuning with BERT", p. 61]. Table 1 (p. 60) counts RE data in relations, not sentences: "DDI, relations 2937 / 1004 / 979"; "ChemProt, relations 4154 / 2416 / 3458" (train/dev/test) [full text, Table 1, p. 60]. These appear to be positive relations only (the DDI test figure 979 equals the positive test instances reported by liu2016drugdrug: "only 7 out of 979 positive instances are wrongly filtered out"); negative candidate pairs are not counted in this table.
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports/frames us. The standard biomedical RE input is already pair-conditioned (one copy of the sentence per candidate pair, the pair blinded); our "pair" arm is in this tradition. Note the difference: BLUE/BioBERT REPLACE the names (anonymise), our pair arm (as we understand it from the brief) names the two organisms. Not a pre-emption: BLUE never compares against a sentence-level (pair-agnostic) classifier.

### lee2020biobert
- Title: BioBERT: a pre-trained biomedical language representation model for biomedical text mining
- Venue: Bioinformatics 36(4):1234-1240, 2020 (published online Sept 2019; Crossref published-print 2020-02-15). DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btz682. PMC7703786.
- Verified at: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz682 (Crossref BibTeX) and full text via Europe PMC (PMC7703786).
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Same pair injection, and it is the source BLUE cites: "We utilized the sentence classifier of the original version of BERT, which uses a [CLS] token for the classification of relations. ... We anonymized target named entities in a sentence using pre-defined tags such as @GENE$ or @DISEASE$. For instance, a sentence with two target entities (gene and disease in this case) is represented as "Serine at position 986 of @GENE$ may be an independent genetic predictor of angiographic @DISEASE$."" [full text, Sec. 3.3 "Fine-tuning BioBERT"]. RE datasets: GAD, EU-ADR (gene-disease), CHEMPROT (protein-chemical) [full text, Sec. 4.1].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports. Establishes that "one [CLS] decision per blinded entity pair" is the default biomedical RE recipe; reviewers will expect us to cite it as the origin of the @GENE$/@DISEASE$ convention.

### zhong2021frustratingly (PURE)
- Title: A Frustratingly Easy Approach for Entity and Relation Extraction
- Venue: Proceedings of NAACL-HLT 2021, pp. 50-61. Anthology 2021.naacl-main.5, DOI 10.18653/v1/2021.naacl-main.5.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.5.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Pair injection by typed markers, one encoder pass per pair: "Our relation model instead processes each pair of spans independently and inserts typed markers at the input layer to highlight the subject and object and their types." [full text, Sec. 3.2, p. 53]. Cost: "One possible shortcoming of our approach is that we need to run our relation model once for every pair of entities." [full text, Sec. 3.3, p. 54]; their batched approximation gives "a 11.9x speedup on ACE05 and a 8.7x speedup on SciERC in the single-sentence setting" while "the relaion [sic] F1 measure decreases by only 1.0% and 1.2% on ACE05 and SciERC respectively" [full text, Sec. 4.3 and Table 3, p. 56]. Most relevant for us, the controlled input ablation (same encoder, only the input/representation changes): "Our key observation is that it is crucial to build different contextual representations for different pairs of spans" and "Compared to TEXT, TYPED MARKERS improved the F1 scores dramatically by +5.0% and +7.4% absolute when gold entities are given." (TEXT = one shared sentence encoding, span-pair representation read off it) [full text, Sec. 5.1, pp. 56-57, and Table 4, p. 57; ACE05 and SciERC dev sets].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Partially pre-empts the PAIR-vs-shared-encoding half: a reviewer will say "pair-specific encodings beat a shared sentence encoding" is known from PURE Sec. 5.1. It does NOT pre-empt our sentence-level question: PURE's TEXT baseline still makes a per-pair decision (span-pair head), never a pair-agnostic sentence decision, and PURE never reads a sentence label off the per-pair scores. Its cost discussion is the honest citation for the price of our pair arm (one pass per candidate pair).

### ye2022packed (PL-Marker; optional, for the cost of per-pair encoding)
- Title: Packed Levitated Marker for Entity and Relation Extraction
- Venue: Proceedings of ACL 2022 (Volume 1: Long Papers), pp. 4904-4917. Anthology 2022.acl-long.337, DOI 10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.337.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.337.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Criticises one-pass-per-pair encoding: "previous work (Zhong and Chen, 2021) has to process span pairs independently, which is time-consuming and ignores the interrelation between the span pairs." [full text, Sec. 2, p. 4906]; packs a subject with all its objects in one pass and "obtains a 4.1%-4.3% strict relation F1 improvement with higher speed over previous state-of-the-art models on ACE04 and ACE05" [abstract].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Tempers. Fully independent per-pair encoding is not the last word: sharing context across the pairs of a sentence can be both faster and more accurate. Cite in limitations/cost discussion if the paper mentions that the pair arm costs one encoder pass per candidate pair.

## Q3(b). QA-style relation extraction (a relation decision as a question about specific entities)

### li2019entity
- Title: Entity-Relation Extraction as Multi-Turn Question Answering
- Venue: Proceedings of ACL 2019, pp. 1340-1350. Anthology P19-1129, DOI 10.18653/v1/P19-1129.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/P19-1129.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Casts end-to-end RE as multi-turn MRC; the relation question is conditioned on ONE previously extracted head entity, and the answer is the tail span or NONE: "At the relation and the tail-entity joint extraction stage, a question is generated by combing a relation-specific template with the extracted head-entity." [full text, Sec. 4.2, p. 1344]; "a relation holds if the returned answer to the question corresponding to that relation is not NONE" [full text, Sec. 1, p. 1341]. Example questions: "which Company / companies did Musk work for?", "when did Musk join SpaceX?" [full text, Sec. 1, p. 1341]. Ablation: natural-language questions beat pseudo-questions on all datasets, e.g. ACE05 relation F1 60.2 vs 58.1 [full text, Sec. 6.1 and Table 9, p. 1346]; "natural language questions provide more fine-grained semantic information" [Sec. 6.1, p. 1346].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports the general idea that a more specific, entity-filled question helps; but the question names one entity (head) plus a relation type, not both members of a pair, and there is no comparison with a pair-agnostic sentence question. Cite as the QA-formulation precedent, not as a pre-emption.

### zhang2023aligning (QA4RE)
- Title: Aligning Instruction Tasks Unlocks Large Language Models as Zero-Shot Relation Extractors
- Venue: Findings of ACL 2023, pp. 794-812. Anthology 2023.findings-acl.50, DOI 10.18653/v1/2023.findings-acl.50.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.50.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Zero-shot LLM RE as multiple-choice QA whose options are relation templates filled with the given head and tail: "By integrating the given head and tail RE entities (Eh and Et) into the relation templates and using them as multiple-choice options, LLMs are able to leverage extensive QA instruction fine-tuning" [full text, Sec. 3.3, p. 797]. Fig. 2 example options: "A. Amanda Knox was born in the city Seattle ... D. Amanda Knox has no known relations to Seattle" followed by "Which option can be inferred from the given sentence?" [full text, Fig. 2, p. 796]. Gain over the vanilla (pair given, label list) prompt: "our framework enables text-davinci-003 and FLAN-T5-XXLarge to achieve an average of 8.2% and 8.6% absolute improvements in F1, respectively" [full text, Sec. 1, p. 795].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports the "ask about the pair" framing for LLMs (pair-filled, natural-language statements to verify). Both its arms are pair-conditioned, so it says nothing about pair vs sentence questions. Useful because our teacher prompt is (as described) a pair-level question to an instruction-tuned LLM.

Reports "a competitive document-level F1 of 71.18% and 93.3%" [abstract].

## Q3(c). LLMs: specific (per-pair) question vs general question; decomposition

### jimenezgutierrez2022thinking
- Title: Thinking about GPT-3 In-Context Learning for Biomedical IE? Think Again
- Venue: Findings of EMNLP 2022, pp. 4497-4512. Anthology 2022.findings-emnlp.329, DOI 10.18653/v1/2022.findings-emnlp.329.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.329.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Biomedical RE (DDI, ChemProt, GAD) with GPT-3 is run per pair ("we add the subject and object entities, in their verbatim lexical form in the original sentence, to the prompt" [full text, Sec. 2.4.1, p. 4499]); the fine-tuned baseline masks the pair ("we mask the object and subject entities in the input sentence and use the [CLS] token" [full text, Sec. 2.3, p. 4498]). Main failure is the null class: "in-context learning struggles with the null class, e.g., sentences that contain no named entity (for NER) or entity pairs that hold none of the target relations (for RE)" [full text, Sec. 1, p. 4498]; "the none relation in DDI is rarely predicted by GPT-3. This bias against the none class greatly degrades the model's precision given that the DDI dataset is, rightfully so, heavily skewed towards this class." [full text, Sec. 4.4.2, p. 4504]. Limitations: "tasks like relation extraction require a more focused view around the target entity pair" [full text, Limitations, p. 4506, about kNN demonstration retrieval].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports two points: (i) negative (co-occurring but non-interacting) pairs dominate biomedical RE data and LLMs over-predict "interaction" for them, which is exactly what a pair verifier must reject; (ii) authors explicitly argue for focusing on the target pair. Does not compare pair vs sentence prompts.

### li2023revisiting (SumAsk)
- Title: Revisiting Large Language Models as Zero-shot Relation Extractors
- Venue: Findings of EMNLP 2023, pp. 6877-6892. Anthology 2023.findings-emnlp.459 (the Anthology .bib gives no DOI).
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.459.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Decomposes the one-step "which relation holds between subject and object?" prompt into per-pair, per-relation yes/no questions ("To classify the relations between the subject and object, a simple method is to sequentially ask LLMs whether each relation exists between two entities." [full text, Sec. 4.2, p. 6879]). Rationale: the vanilla prompt "makes LLMs to accomplish three no-trivial reasoning processes in only one step" [full text, Sec. 4.1, p. 6879]. Result: "SUMASK consistently and significantly improves LLMs performance by 5.2% - 48.3% in F1-score compared to VANILLA prompting w.r.t. diverse experimental settings." [full text, Sec. 1, contributions, p. 6878]. Cost acknowledged: complexity "O(k x r x n)" because it must "enumerate all possible triples" [full text, Appendix E "Discussions", p. 6888].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports "a narrower, more specific question is easier than a general one" for LLM RE (decomposing a relation-label question into specific yes/no questions about the pair). It decomposes over relation types, not over entity pairs, and does not test sentence-level decisions; cite as analogous evidence only.

### rehana2024evaluating
- Title: Evaluating GPT and BERT models for protein-protein interaction identification in biomedical text
- Venue: Bioinformatics Advances 4(1):vbae133, 2024. DOI 10.1093/bioadv/vbae133. PMC11419952.
- Verified at: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioadv/vbae133 (Crossref BibTeX); full text via Europe PMC (PMC11419952).
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Zero-shot GPT-3.5/GPT-4 on LLL, IEPA, HPRD50 with two input styles: (1) whole-sentence extraction, base prompt "Extract every pair of PPI from the sentences provided as input" [full text, Sec. 2.3, Table 3]; (2) PROTEIN-masked per-pair inputs as for BERT ("we replaced the entity pair names with the PROTEIN1 and PROTEIN2 keywords. Other than the pair, the protein names in the sentence were substituted with the PROTEIN keyword. ... sentences were repeated with the placeholders ... to represent different protein pairs within the same sentence" [full text, Sec. 2.2]). On IEPA the best GPT setting is per-pair: "GPT-4 with PROTEIN masking (one sentence at a time) has the highest recall (79.41%) and F1 score (71.54%) for IEPA dataset" [full text, Sec. 3.3]; on HPRD50 "GPT-4 with PROTEIN masking (one sentence at a time) impressively achieves the best recall of 95.22%" [Sec. 3.3]; Discussion: "the models work better on IEPA and HPRD50 in the case of PROTEIN masked settings compared to original sentences" [full text, Sec. 4]. On LLL the best GPT-4 result came from an original-sentence setting: "For LLL dataset, GPT-4, especially when enhanced with a Protein dictionary, exhibits remarkable strengths. It stands out with its precision rate of 88.37%" [full text, Sec. 3.3]; the dictionary settings use "the original sentences as the input" [full text, Sec. 4]; abstract: "GPT-4 achieved the highest precision of 88.37%, a recall of 85.14%, and an F1 score of 86.49%" on LLL [abstract]. Per-setting tables are in the supplement (not read).
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: The closest biomedical-interaction evidence that an LLM asked about a designated pair does better than one asked to find all interacting pairs in the sentence, on the two corpora with many non-interacting pairs (IEPA, HPRD50) but not on LLL. Confounded (masking also hides names; dictionary arms differ); cite as consistent, not as a controlled test. Also gives corpus pair counts (Q5).

### mraz2026fewshot (arXiv preprint, no peer-reviewed version found)
- Title: Few-Shot Biomedical Relation Extraction with Large Language Models: A Viable Alternative to Supervised Learning?
- Venue: arXiv:2606.15412 (v2, 3 Aug 2026). Authors Mraz, Curk, Zupan. Preprint; no peer-reviewed version found (Semantic Scholar lists only arXiv, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2606.15412; Crossref search found nothing). Its format (LNCS-style) suggests a conference submission; re-check before camera-ready.
- Verified at: https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=2606.15412 and PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15412v2
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Directly compares, with the same LLMs on BioREDirect, "pairwise classification, which predicts relations for individual entity pairs, and joint generation, which extracts multiple relations in a single model call" [abstract]. "Pairwise classification achieves higher recall, whereas joint generation is more precise and computationally efficient." [abstract]. "pairwise classification tends to overinterpret context, whereas joint generation is primarily limited by incomplete relation coverage" [full text, Sec. 4, p. 10]. Joint generation reduces "computational costs by up to 25x compared to pairwise classification" [full text, Sec. 4, p. 11]. Conclusion: "classification and generation achieved broadly comparable F1 scores, with performance differences primarily reflecting a precision-recall trade-off rather than a clear superiority of either approach" [full text, Sec. 5].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Contrasts / tempers. For LLM prompting, asking per pair is not uniformly better than asking for all pairs at once (precision drops). Note the comparison is per-pair vs joint pair extraction, not per-pair vs a sentence-level yes/no question, so it does not refute our claim; but we must not claim LLM per-pair prompting is known to dominate.

### zhang2025entity (EP-RSR; general domain, LLM document-level RE)
- Title: Entity Pair-guided Relation Summarization and Retrieval in LLMs for Document-level Relation Extraction
- Venue: Findings of NAACL 2025, pp. 4022-4037. Anthology 2025.findings-naacl.224, DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-naacl.224.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.224.bib and PDF.
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Contrasts asking the LLM at document level with asking per entity pair: "Current LLM-based methods typically focus on filtering all potential relations (candidate relations) within a document at one time ... However, most approaches for candidate relation filtering are based on the document level, which results in insufficient correlation between candidate relations and entity pairs." [abstract]. Controlled ablation on DocRED dev: "Replacing the entit-pair-level candidate relations with the document-level candidate relations, the performance of our model experiences a significant drop, with F1 and Ign F1 dropping by 6.77 and 5.68 respectively" [full text, Sec. 4.3, p. 4029]; on the filtering step alone, Table 5: entity-pair-level P/R/F1 23.46 / 91.24 / 37.33 vs document-level 17.81 / 78.78 / 29.05 [full text, Sec. 4.5, Table 5, p. 4029]. Differences: general domain (DocRED), document scope, the per-pair query is about candidate relation types (not existence), and the decision being measured is pair-level, not a sentence-level decision. Not a pre-emption of the sentence-level result.

## Q5 (biomedical). Sentences that describe an interaction also contain non-interacting pairs

### pyysalo2008comparative
- Title: Comparative analysis of five protein-protein interaction corpora
- Venue: BMC Bioinformatics 9(Suppl 3):S6, 2008. DOI 10.1186/1471-2105-9-S3-S6. PMC2349296.
- Verified at: https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-9-S3-S6 (Crossref BibTeX); full text and tables via Europe PMC (PMC2349296).
- WHAT IT SHOWS [full text, tables read in the PMC XML]:
  - Table 1 ("Corpora"), row "size" (sentences): AIMed 1955, BioInfer 1100, HPRD50 145, IEPA 486, LLL 77.
  - Table 3 ("Corpus statistics"), per-sentence averages (Entity pairs / Interactions) and "Fraction of sentences with No interactions": AIMed 3.0 / 0.5 / 69%; BioInfer 9.4 / 1.3 / 48%; HPRD50 3.0 / 1.1 / 38%; IEPA 1.7 / 0.7 / 37%; LLL 4.3 / 2.1 / 0%.
  - Text, Sec. "Corpus statistics": "the average number of interactions divided by the average number of entity pairs per sentence (below abbreviated I/EP) equals the precision of the co-occurrence method evaluated above." and "As more proteins are annotated, we would not expect I to grow more than linearly, while EP grows quadratically."
  - Table 2 ("PPI extraction performance"), co-occurrence (all pairs in a sentence) P / R / F: AIMed 0.17 / 0.95 / 0.29; BioInfer 0.13 / 0.99 / 0.23; HPRD50 0.38 / 1.0 / 0.55; IEPA 0.41 / 1.0 / 0.58; LLL 0.50 / 1.0 / 0.66.
  - Table 4 ("PPI extraction performance on filtered corpora"), caption: "Precision and F-score for the co-occurrence and RelEx methods on the corpora with only entities that participate in an interaction preserved. Recall is not shown as it is unaffected by this modification." Co-occurrence P after filtering: AIMed 0.53, BioInfer 0.53, HPRD50 0.64, IEPA 0.88, LLL 0.50. Text: "Remarkably, filtering also causes the simple co-occurrence method to outperform the more advanced method on all corpora except LLL, where no entities are filtered."
- KEY NUMBERS FOR OUR ARGUMENT:
  1. LLL is a corpus in which every sentence contains an interaction (Table 3: 0% of sentences with no interactions), yet proposing every co-occurring pair gives pair precision 0.50 (Table 2). This is a published, direct instance of "a perfect sentence filter has limited pair precision" (our benchmark: 0.724).
  2. Our inference (not stated by the authors; please check the reasoning before using): entity filtering (Table 4) removes all pairs in sentences without interactions plus pairs involving non-interacting proteins inside interaction sentences; every removed pair is negative and recall is unchanged (caption). A perfect sentence-level filter keeps a superset of those pairs. Hence pair precision after a perfect sentence filter is at most the Table 4 co-occurrence P: 0.53 (AIMed), 0.53 (BioInfer), 0.64 (HPRD50), 0.88 (IEPA), 0.50 (LLL). Exact sentence-filtered values are not reported.
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Strong support for "only pair-level answers can populate a pair database": even knowing exactly which sentences describe an interaction leaves at least 12% (IEPA) to 50% (LLL) of co-occurring pairs non-interacting in these corpora (1 minus the Table 4 co-occurrence precision; lower bounds by the inference above). It also pre-empts the observation itself (co-occurrence precision = I/EP), so the paper should cite it as known rather than claim it.

### airola2008allpaths
- Title: All-paths graph kernel for protein-protein interaction extraction with evaluation of cross-corpus learning
- Venue: BMC Bioinformatics 9(Suppl 11):S2, 2008. DOI 10.1186/1471-2105-9-S11-S2. PMC2586751.
- Verified at: https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-9-S11-S2 (Crossref BibTeX); full text and tables via Europe PMC (PMC2586751).
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Table 1 ("Evaluation results. Counts of positive and negative examples in the corpora ...") [full text, Table 1], #POS / #NEG (candidate pairs): AIMed 1000 / 4834; BioInfer 2534 / 7132; HPRD50 163 / 270; IEPA 335 / 482; LLL 164 / 166. ### tikk2013detailed
- Title: A detailed error analysis of 13 kernel methods for protein-protein interaction extraction
- Venue: BMC Bioinformatics 14:12, 2013. DOI 10.1186/1471-2105-14-12. PMC3680070.
- Verified at: https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-14-12 (Crossref BibTeX); full text via Europe PMC (PMC3680070).
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Pair-level PPI corpora (AIMed, BioInfer, HPRD50, IEPA, LLL); candidate pairs come from sentences "some of which contain PPIs and/or non-interacting proteins" [full text, Background]. Key qualitative finding: "We can see that the more protein mentions a sentence exhibits, the lower the ratio of positive pairs. This is consistent with the previous experiment on PD pairs: in long sentences there are only few positive pairs, and those are difficult to classify." and "the number of pairs increases quadratically with the number of mentions" [full text, Results and discussion, subsection "Relation between sentence length, entity distance and pair difficulty", Figure 7]. Numbers are only in figures (not quoted).
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: Supports our BioRED observation that pair conditioning helps only on sentences with more than two entities: the share of non-interacting pairs grows with entity count, which is exactly where a sentence-level filter loses pair precision.

### polajnar2011protein (sentence-level PPI detection, the alternative we compare against)
- Title: Protein interaction sentence detection using multiple semantic kernels
- Venue: Journal of Biomedical Semantics 2:1, 2011. DOI 10.1186/2041-1480-2-1. PMC3116455.
- Verified at: https://doi.org/10.1186/2041-1480-2-1 (Crossref BibTeX); full text via Europe PMC (PMC3116455).
- WHAT IT SHOWS: Explicitly chooses the sentence-level formulation on AIMed over the pair-level one and states its cost: pair-level work uses interacting pairs as positives "while any two proteins, that occur in the same sentence and do not interact, constitute the negative data. The other approach, and one which is employed in this paper, is to consider the sentences that contain interactions as positive examples, and the ones that do not, as negative. ... The simpler classification leads to higher precision and recall, but only locates the sentence that describes the PPI and not the exact interacting pair." and "the data set contains 614 positive and 1355 negative sentences" [full text, Experimental Setup].
- HOW OUR CLAIM RELATES: The clearest biomedical precedent for a direct sentence-level interaction classifier, with the explicit belief that the sentence task is simpler and the explicit admission that it cannot give the pair. Our controlled comparison tests that belief (sentence decision read off pair verification vs a direct sentence classifier). Supports "only pair-level answers populate a pair database" and contrasts with the "sentence-level is easier" assumption (their claim is not a controlled comparison).

## Claims a reviewer would call known, and the citation that makes them known
1. Biomedical RE is posed per candidate pair, with the pair blinded or marked in a copy of the sentence: peng2019transfer (Sec. 4.1.2), lee2020biobert (Sec. 3.3); markers: soares2019matching, zhou2022improved (already cited), zhong2021frustratingly.
2. Building a separate, pair-specific encoding beats reading the pair off one shared sentence encoding (same encoder): zhong2021frustratingly, Sec. 5.1 / Table 4 (+5.0 and +7.4 F1 with gold entities).
3. Per-pair encoding costs one forward pass per candidate pair, and this can be amortised: zhong2021frustratingly Sec. 3.3 (8-16x speedup approximation), ye2022packed.
4. All-pairs co-occurrence has low pair precision, equal to interactions per sentence over entity pairs per sentence (I/EP); much of corpus difficulty comes from non-interacting annotated entities: pyysalo2008comparative (Tables 2-4).
5. Non-interacting pairs outnumber interacting ones in PPI and DDI corpora: airola2008allpaths Table 1; rehana2024evaluating Table 2; liu2016drugdrug Table 2.
6. The more entities a sentence names, the lower its share of positive pairs: tikk2013detailed (Fig. 7 discussion). This makes our "gain only on sentences with more than two entities" result expected in direction (but not in size).
7. Sentence-level interaction detection exists as its own (assumed simpler) task, and it cannot return the pair: polajnar2011protein.
8. Phrasing the relation decision as a natural-language question/hypothesis about the given entities helps, especially zero-shot: levy2017zero, sainz2021label, obamuyide2018zero (cited), li2019entity, zhang2023aligning; decomposing into narrower yes/no questions helps LLMs: li2023revisiting; pair-level queries beat document-level queries in an LLM DocRE pipeline: zhang2025entity.
9. LLMs over-predict relations for co-occurring but unrelated pairs (null class): jimenezgutierrez2022thinking.
10. Fine-tuned small encoders beat zero/few-shot LLMs on biomedical RE (motivates distilling the teacher): chen2025benchmarking, jimenezgutierrez2022thinking.

## Claims we must not make, and the work that refutes or qualifies each
1. "Asking an LLM about one pair at a time is known to be better than asking for all pairs at once." Qualified/contradicted by mraz2026fewshot (pairwise: higher recall, lower precision, "broadly comparable F1", joint up to 25x cheaper; arXiv) and labonte2026structured (multi-extraction "improves performance by up to 19%" over single extraction; abstract only). Our claim is about a fine-tuned encoder and a sentence-level decision; keep it there.
2. "Pair-conditioned input is our contribution." It is the default biomedical RE recipe: peng2019transfer, lee2020biobert, zhong2021frustratingly (and cited soares2019matching, zhou2022improved). The contribution is the controlled sentence-vs-pair measurement with the same teacher, data and encoder.
3. "We are the first to show that knowing which sentences describe an interaction does not give the interacting pairs." pyysalo2008comparative (co-occurrence precision = I/EP; LLL 0% interaction-free sentences, precision 0.50) and polajnar2011protein (sentence detection "only locates the sentence ... and not the exact interacting pair") make the point known; present our 0.724 as a measurement on the biodiversity benchmark, citing them.
4. "Independent per-pair encoding is the best design." ye2022packed reports packing pairs that share a subject is faster and +4.1-4.3 strict relation F1 on ACE04/05.
5. "QA4RE / SumAsk show that pair questions beat sentence questions." They do not: every arm in those papers is already pair-conditioned (QA4RE varies the format; SumAsk decomposes over relation types).
6. "BLUE Table 1 gives candidate-pair counts." Those are relation counts (apparently positives only; DDI test 979 = positive instances per liu2016drugdrug).
7. Do not mix co-occurrence precisions across papers: airola2008allpaths Table 1 (e.g. LLL 55.9%, BioInfer 26.6%) differs from pyysalo2008comparative Table 2 (LLL 0.50, BioInfer 0.13) because of corpus versions/conversions.
8. "A published statistic gives the share of negative pairs inside sentences with at least one positive pair." I found none (see Q5); only the LLL case and the Table 4 upper bounds.
