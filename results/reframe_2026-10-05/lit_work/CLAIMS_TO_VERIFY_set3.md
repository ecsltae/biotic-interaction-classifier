rei2019jointly | AAAI 2019 "Jointly Learning to Label Sentences and Tokens": same data and BiLSTM encoder; adding token-level labels raises sentence-level F1 on all four tasks (BiLSTM-ATTN 83.87/85.21/89.77/93.96 vs +token 85.90/85.41/91.97/95.82 on CoNLL-2010, FCE, SST-neg, SST-pos) | Table 3
dasanmartino2019fine | EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019 propaganda: SLC "asks to predict whether a sentence contains at least one propaganda technique"; same corpus and BERT, sentence-only BERT F1 57.74 vs multi-granularity 60.98; fine-grained level "makes it possible to explain to the user why an article was judged as propagandistic" | Sec. 5, Table 7, Sec. 6.3, Sec. 8
zhang2016rationale | EMNLP 2016 RA-CNN: sentence-level rationale supervision improves document classification over Doc-CNN on the same data (e.g. movie reviews 87.14 -> 90.43) and provides explanations | abstract, Tables 2-3
farkas2010conll | CoNLL-2010 shared task: "sentences containing at least one cue were considered as uncertain"; on biological text the top systems used sequence labelling of cues (sentence uncertain if at least one cue found), on Wikipedia bag-of-words sentence classifiers won | task definition, Sec. 6.2, Tables
li2021dual | CVPR 2021 DSMIL: a fully supervised patch model (pixel-level annotations, slide score = mean of patch scores) is the upper bound: Camelyon16 AUC 0.9362 vs max-pooling MIL 0.8641 and DSMIL 0.8944 | Sec. 4.1, Table 1
hershey2021benefit | ICASSP 2021: same clips, same model; strong (temporally precise) labels vs the same labels spread over the clip; clip-level d' 0.82 (diffuse) vs 0.96 (strong); "The only difference between +Diffuse and +Strong is the temporal precision" | abstract, Sec. 4, Table 1
magge2021deepademiner | JAMIA 2021: on the same tweet corpus, a RoBERTa tweet classifier (F1 0.63) beats the ADE span tagger read as "any span found" (F1 0.41) for "does the tweet contain an ADE?" | Results, Experiment 2
pavlopoulos2022from | ACL 2022 toxic spans: rationale extraction on top of a post-level toxicity classifier is "surprisingly promising" and can approach a span-trained sequence labeller | abstract, Sec. 6, Conclusions
hu2025decomposition | NAACL 2025 "Decomposition Dilemmas": claim decomposition "generally benefits weaker verifiers, while it tends to negatively affect stronger verification systems"; effect depends on input granularity | abstract, Sec. 4.1-4.2
dekok2018review | Progress in AI 2018: same SemEval-2016 data, a review-level aspect-polarity classifier beats summing sentence-level predictions ("the pure review-level algorithm outperforms the sentence aggregation method") | abstract, Tables 4-5
wadden2022multivers | Findings of NAACL 2022 MultiVerS: rationales "may lack information required to make a prediction when taken out-of-context"; pipeline/MT-PI approaches degrade more on context-dependent instances than the full-document multitask model | Sec. 1, Sec. 7.1, Table 3
zaidan2007using | NAACL-HLT 2007: annotator rationales used during training improve movie-review sentiment classification; "providing rationales is a more fruitful use of an annotator's time than annotating more examples" | abstract
